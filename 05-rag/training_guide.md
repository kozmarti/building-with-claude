# Club Endurance Training Guide

A made-up guide for a small running and cycling club, written as test
material for this RAG module. It is not medical or coaching advice.

## 1. Training zones

The club uses five heart rate zones, set from each athlete's maximum heart
rate. Zone 1 (under 60%) is very easy recovery. Zone 2 (60–70%) is the
conversational pace where most weekly volume should happen. Zone 3 (70–80%)
is steady "tempo" effort. Zone 4 (80–90%) is threshold, sustainable for
roughly 20 to 40 minutes. Zone 5 (above 90%) is short, hard intervals.
As a rule, about 80% of weekly training time should be in zones 1 and 2.

## 2. Workout library

Every planned session has a code so coaches and athletes mean the same thing.

- **Z2-LONG:** the weekly long run or ride, fully in zone 2. Runners build
  from 60 to 120 minutes; cyclists from 2 to 4 hours.
- **TEMPO-20:** 15 minutes easy, 20 minutes in zone 3, 10 minutes easy.
- **THR-3x10:** three 10-minute blocks in zone 4 with 3 minutes easy jogging
  between them.
- **VO2-5x4:** five 4-minute intervals in zone 5 with 3 minutes of easy
  recovery. Only for athletes with at least 8 weeks of consistent training.
- **STRIDES-6:** six 20-second relaxed accelerations after an easy run.

No athlete should do more than two of THR-3x10 and VO2-5x4 in the same week.

## 3. Building weekly volume

Increase weekly training time by no more than about 10% from one week to the
next. Every fourth week is a recovery week: cut volume by 30 to 40% and drop
the hardest session. After an illness of more than three days, restart at
half the previous volume and build back over two weeks.

## 4. Fuelling during sessions

Sessions under 75 minutes need no food, only water. For longer sessions, aim
for 30 to 60 grams of carbohydrate per hour, starting after 30 to 45 minutes.
For events over 2.5 hours, trained athletes can use up to 90 grams per hour,
but only if they have practised it in training first. Never try a new gel or
drink on race day.

## 5. Hydration

Drink to thirst on most days. In heat above 25 °C, weigh yourself before and
after a long session: each kilogram lost is about one litre of fluid to
replace, plus some sodium. Sessions over 90 minutes in the heat need a drink
with electrolytes, not plain water only.

## 6. Recovery and sleep

Sleep is the most important recovery tool: the club recommends 7 to 9 hours.
Within an hour after a hard or long session, eat a meal or snack with both
carbohydrate and around 20 to 30 grams of protein. Leave at least 48 hours
between two hard sessions (THR-3x10, VO2-5x4 or a race).

## 7. Warning signs of overtraining

Stop hard training and talk to a coach if you notice several of these for more
than a week: morning resting heart rate 5 or more beats above normal, poor
sleep, heavy legs on easy runs, loss of motivation, or frequent small
illnesses. Pain that changes how you run or ride is a reason to stop and see a
health professional, not to push through.

## 8. Taper before a race

Reduce volume for the last 10 to 14 days before a goal race: about 20% less
in the second-to-last week and 40 to 50% less in the final week. Keep a few
short efforts at race pace so the legs stay sharp, for example STRIDES-6 or a
shortened TEMPO-20. Do not add new workouts during the taper.

## 9. Club race calendar rules

Members can enter at most one goal race (marathon, half-Ironman or 200 km
sportive) every 12 weeks. Club entries for the spring half marathon close on
1 March. Members who want a club place in an event must have attended at least
ten club sessions in the previous three months.
